## Post 06: How the adaptive Digital SAT works

Image: post_06.png

The Digital SAT is a multistage adaptive test, and that changes how students should approach it.

Each section is split into two modules. Performance in the first module determines whether the second module is easier or harder. The harder second module is where the highest scores become possible.

The practical lesson: accuracy in module 1 matters a great deal. Rushing early questions to bank time can quietly cap a student's score.

We train students to treat module 1 with care rather than speed.

## Post 07: There is no penalty for guessing on the SAT

Image: post_07.png

A surprising number of students still leave questions blank on the SAT.

There is no penalty for a wrong answer. A blank scores zero; a guess has a chance of scoring. Every question should get an answer.

The better habit: eliminate the choices you know are wrong, pick the best remaining option, flag the question in Bluebook, and come back if time allows.

Small habits like this protect points that students have already earned.

## Post 08: Superscoring, explained for parents

Image: post_08.png

Superscoring is one of the most useful policies families do not know about.

Many colleges take a student's highest Reading and Writing score and highest Math score, even if they came from different test dates, and combine them.

That means a second or third attempt can be focused. A student strong in Math but weaker in Reading and Writing can spend the next round of preparation almost entirely on the weaker section.

Policies vary by college, so check each one, but plan your test dates with this in mind.

## Post 25: Common SAT Math traps

Image: post_25.png

Many lost points in SAT Math are not content gaps. They are traps.

The most common: solving for x when the question asks for something else, ignoring a condition such as x is negative, reversing a percentage incorrectly after a discount, and losing track of units.

The fix is a habit: before choosing an answer, reread exactly what the question asks. We drill this until it becomes automatic.

## Post 26: A three-step method for words in context

Image: post_26.png

Vocabulary questions on the Digital SAT test meaning in context, not memorized definitions.

A reliable method: cover the answer choices, read the sentence, and predict your own word based on the passage. Then choose the option that matches your prediction most closely.

This stops attractive but wrong choices from steering the student, and it works even when the words themselves are unfamiliar.

## Post 27: Transition words the SAT tests most

Image: post_27.png

Transition questions are among the most learnable on the SAT.

Each transition signals a relationship between ideas: contrast, result, example or addition. The method is to identify the relationship between the two sentences first, then choose the word that names it.

Students who learn to see the logic between sentences answer these quickly and accurately.

## Post 43: Punctuation rules that cost SAT points

Image: post_43.png

Punctuation questions are some of the most predictable on the SAT.

A handful of rules cover most of them: semicolons join complete sentences, colons follow a complete sentence, non-essential information is set off by a pair of commas, and two sentences cannot be joined with just a comma.

Learn the rules once, practice them well, and these become reliable points.
